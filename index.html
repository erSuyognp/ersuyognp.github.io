PRJ-01Haptic Focus AssistantDemoed · Hardware built
Wearable hardware · EE 3240 Junior Design

Haptic Focus Assistant

A BLE wrist wearable that interrupts phone distraction with a buzz instead of a block. App blockers are rigid and easy to bypass; this takes the opposite approach — a Kotlin Android app watches foreground app usage through UsageStatsManager, and the moment a flagged app opens it fires a BLE GATT write to the band, which answers with a 4 kHz siren, a pulsing coin motor, and a blinking red LED. You feel the habit instead of being locked out of it.

On the wearable side, a XIAO ESP32-C3 runs a four-state machine — idle, connected, alerting, cooldown — driving an ERM coin motor through a 2N2222 BJT and a piezo buzzer in push-pull off two GPIO pins. Two problems shaped the final design: the BS170 MOSFET I specified first wouldn't saturate at a 3.3 V gate, so the motor heated without turning until I swapped in the BJT; and the blue LED channel had to be dropped from firmware because D0 sits on an ESP32-C3 strapping pin that's pulled high during boot. Driving the buzzer push-pull doubled the swing to 6.6 Vpp without adding a driver transistor at all.

PRJ-02Iron GloveHopHacks 2026 · Winner
Hackathon · CesiumJS / SpacetimeDB

Iron Glove

★ HopHacks 2026 · Winner

A multiplayer Iron Man flight game rendered over the real JHU Homewood campus in CesiumJS with Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles. You fly the suit with full six-axis controls — thrust and brake, yaw, climb and dive, barrel rolls, and boost — while real buildings and terrain stop the suit cold and a PULL UP warning flashes below ~45 m. Let go of everything and it hovers in place.

Multiplayer runs on SpacetimeDB, the track it was built for: each pilot's state lives in one shared player_state table that the server broadcasts to every other client, so you can switch cameras between pilots sharing the same sky. Optional layers stack on top — a JARVIS voice through ElevenLabs, natural-language flight commands routed through Grok, and a reinforcement-learning "Sentinel" drone mission.

PRJ-03BuddyHackRice 16
Hackathon · Python / ML / embedded · HackRice 16

Buddy

An AI productivity mentor that watches what you are actually doing, decides when you have drifted, and says something about it out loud. A blocklist can't tell a linear-algebra lecture on YouTube from sketch comedy on the same site, so people just disable it. Buddy classifies the content instead: its model learned that the word lecture outweighs the domain youtube, so on one page it stays quiet and on the other it speaks up. It never sees your screen — only the domain and the tab title.

A Chrome extension turns browsing into focus sessions and heartbeats them to a FastAPI engine, which vectorizes each with TF-IDF and scores it with logistic regression into a productivity probability. That moves a time-weighted 0-to-1 score whose trend streams to MATLAB, which returns a nudge level; Gemini writes the line, ElevenLabs voices it, and an ESP32-S3 watch polls every two seconds and plays it.

Recall on the productive class is favored on purpose — interrupting someone who is genuinely working is the expensive mistake — so the honest by-template split reports about 0.925 accuracy with recall pinned near 1.0. Storage is managed Postgres (TigerData), seven plain tables, no hypertables the row count never justified.

PRJ-04FPGA Options Strategy Engine188 MHz Fmax · CI verified
Digital design · SystemVerilog / Artix-7

FPGA Options Strategy Engine

A hardware options trading engine on an Artix-7, built to find out how much of a trading decision can be made in silicon before software ever sees the packet. A six-stage pipeline decodes a raw ITCH 5.0 market feed byte-serially, caches option legs, evaluates three spread strategies, clears six pre-trade risk checks, and emits an OUCH 4.2 order — 17 clock cycles tick-to-trade, about 90 ns at a 188 MHz Fmax.

Everything runs in integer fixed-point. Strategy evaluation compares option payoffs using a cross-multiplication identity so the design never instantiates a hardware divider, and a priority-encoder scheduler walks a dirty bitmap so only legs whose quotes actually moved get re-evaluated. The leg cache hashes ticker symbols with an XOR-rotate — plain byte-XOR collapsed almost every symbol into the same few buckets, because ASCII capital letters share their high bits.

Getting to 188 MHz was a synthesis lesson: my first memory read was combinational, which blocked BRAM inference and capped Fmax around 119 MHz. Splitting the read into issue and compare stages let Vivado infer block RAM properly. Verified against a Python golden model with cocotb and Verilator, running in GitHub Actions on every push.

PRJ-05SparkLive · Cloudflare Workers
Full stack · Cloudflare Workers

Spark

Paste a course syllabus and Spark turns every topic into a question you can't stop thinking about, with the answer deliberately withheld until you commit to a guess. The idea came from two teachers: one who had us work out a building's height from its shadow before teaching any trigonometry, and one who asked whether a sealed room gets warmer or colder when you switch on an air conditioner, then refused to answer until the lecture was over. Nobody forgets a question they got wrong. Built for the PathFinder's Challenge.

The engineering that makes it viable is the caching. Cards are keyed by topic rather than by syllabus, so two students whose documents are worded nothing alike but both cover the second law of thermodynamics share one generated card — and courses repeat enormously. Parsing runs on a cheap model, since stripping office hours and grading policy is mechanical; the cards themselves run on a stronger one, because specificity is the entire product. A full eight-topic syllabus costs $0.17, measured against the live API rather than estimated.

Spend is metered in microdollars from the tokens each response actually reports, against both a daily and a lifetime ceiling; past a cap the API degrades to pre-written samples while cached cards keep serving. Roughly one card in five truncates mid-JSON because adaptive thinking shares its token budget with the answer, so the Worker walks a three-rung fallback ladder down to thinking disabled, where output is bounded and can't truncate. 81 assertions cover routing, cache keying, spend caps, and the guarantee that raw syllabus text never reaches the public gallery.

PRJ-06Biased Coin DeskLive simulator
Interactive simulation · Web

Biased Coin Desk

A Kelly criterion sandbox built on the Haghani & Dewey experiment: 61 finance students were each given $25 and a coin weighted 60% toward heads. About a third of them still went broke. The edge was never the problem — the bet sizing was.

The simulator lets you flip that coin under six sizing strategies — full, half, and quarter Kelly, fixed fraction, fixed dollar, and martingale — while tracking bankroll, rolling volatility, drawdown, and losing streaks in real time. I extended the original study with variable risk-to-reward ratios, a calendar time axis, and a thousand-lifetime Monte Carlo run that shows how often each strategy ends in ruin.
